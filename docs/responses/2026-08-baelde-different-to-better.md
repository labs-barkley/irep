# Response to Baelde (2026), "From 'Different' to 'Better'"

Elodie Aishwarya P. Remoissenet, initial steward. August 2026. Status: living document, revised as the linked issues close.

Reference: Baelde, M. (2026). From "Different" to "Better": What It Would Take to Show That a Hiring Protocol Works. Zenodo working paper, draft for public comment. DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22112323. CC BY 4.0.

The note is the strongest external review this protocol has received, and most of it is adopted rather than answered. IREP improves in public; this document records what changes, what is decided, and where the disagreement is real.

## Adopted as stated

The Stage 1 ceiling: retrospective re-scoring identifies divergence and composition and will never identify value, because the informative half of the disagreement region is empty by construction. Stage 1 claims are rescoped accordingly (issue #14). The two exploration designs and their distinct estimands, with budget reserved for score-independent advancement and regression discontinuity recorded as the weaker fallback (issue #11). The outcome hierarchy, with 12-month survival split by separation type as primary and ratings demoted to secondary (issue #12). The differential prediction test added to the Stage 4 audit, with its collider caveat (issue #13). The power arithmetic, verified independently and adopted: firm-level retention evidence is out of reach, and Stage 3 is pre-registered to claim divergence, composition and process metrics only. The registry's three preconditions: k-anonymity of trajectory fields, joint controllership written before the first record, externally timestamped operational pre-registration (issue #15). And the operational triad for any adopting firm, paraphrased into the implementation guide: pre-register rubric, threshold and primary outcome per requisition with an external timestamp; log every score and every decision, rejections included, with the borderline band flagged; run exploration at whatever rate is affordable. Data collected without the second and third is not analysable at any volume.

## Decided in response

The note's first objection, the aggregation gap, demanded a decision rather than an acknowledgment. The decision, open for comment in issue #10: the evaluation layer is role-referenced with an absolute pre-registered threshold per requisition, and unfilled requisitions are accepted when nobody clears it. The allocation layer, when several candidates clear the threshold for fewer openings, is comparative by arithmetic necessity, and the protocol's commitment there is that the comparison runs on evidence, never on categories. The glossary claim that the population is an audit instrument is rescoped to the evaluation layer. This choice is the one consistent with P1, and it is what makes a borderline band definable, which the note correctly identifies as the precondition of every measurement design downstream.

## Where the disagreement is real

The note's corollary in §2.3: that tight item anchoring does the work, more than the change of referential from population to role. As stated this is a hypothesis, and the note itself supplies the reason: the two levers are separable and separately measurable, and the two-by-two of masking crossed with pre-registered rubric has never been run. IREP's position is to take the note at its word and put the decomposition into the pre-registration rather than concede the corollary in advance. One interaction argues against full orthogonality: the referential decides which information reaches the evaluator, and the compensatory mechanism documented in the French anonymous-CV experiment, the contextual reading of atypical trajectories, depends on exactly that information. A rubric anchored on trajectory-rich, role-referenced items is not the same instrument as the same rubric anchored on a masked file.

## One addition

The Stage 1 ceiling assumes a strictly deterministic historical decision rule. Real pipelines often are not: multiple screeners, quasi-random file assignment, unequal severities. Where screener identity was logged and assignment quasi-random, examiner-severity designs identify a local effect on marginal candidates, because the selection propensity is then strictly between 0 and 1 on part of the disagreement region. This does not remove the ceiling; it cracks it on historical data meeting those conditions, and it strengthens the note's own conclusion that logging is the decision that cannot be recovered later.

## Scope note

The real-data crossing-point stage in VALIDATION.md tests the estimator claim: whether an individual baseline outperforms a reference class in out-of-sample prediction, and at what number of observations. The note addresses the causal claim: whether hiring under the protocol produces better hires. These are complementary and neither substitutes for the other.
